Maison Noor

Oud and amber, in deep glass

Maison Noor works the way the old attar makers did: small batches, oils rested before they are blended, nothing hurried. Their oud is aged in cedar until its sharp edges turn smooth and resinous, then set against saffron and Taif rose.

These are perfumes for weddings, Eid evenings and winter dinners, and they are still on a shawl the next morning.

Velluto Nero

Leather, cedar and vetiver, in matte black

Velluto Nero is a small studio that only works in the dark register: suede, smoked birch, Atlas cedar, Haitian vetiver. Their compositions are cut close and dry, nearer to a tailored coat than a cologne, and nothing in the range is sweet.

The bottle is heavy in the hand and deliberately plain. The scent does the talking.

Aurelle

Rose, jasmine and peony, in faceted glass

Aurelle composes florals with the windows open. Rose, jasmine and peony are handled lightly, with a little citrus on top and soft musk underneath, so the flowers smell freshly picked rather than preserved.

They suit long office days, garden lunches and summer weddings, and the faceted bottle throws light across a dressing table.

Sahara Royale

Citrus and sea salt, in pale teal glass

Sahara Royale bottles the cool side of heat: bergamot and grapefruit, a breath of sea salt and a thread of vetiver. These are the scents we reach for in a Karachi June or on a long motorway drive north, when anything heavier would be too much.

The pale teal glass is meant to look like shallow water. It is the house most people wear every day.

How we choose our houses

We carry four houses, not forty. A perfumer earns a place on our shelves only when all three of these are true.

It has a voice of its own
Every fragrance in the range is recognisably theirs. We would rather stock three remarkable perfumes than a dozen near misses.
It sells to us directly
Bottles come to us sealed from the house itself, with no importer or reseller in between, so we can stand behind every one.
It wears well here
We wear each scent through a Pakistani summer and a winter before it goes on sale. Heat changes a perfume, and we want to know how.
